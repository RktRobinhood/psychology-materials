Upload index.html to the folder you want students to visit. No build step, teacher HTML, separate review tool, account, external font or CDN is needed. The two text files in this ZIP are documentation and software notices. All required code, CSS, fonts, QR generation and PDF generation are embedded in index.html.

Keep the page available at that address while reviewing submissions. A QR generated from a local file carries plain text instead; a QR generated from a local development preview points to that preview.

PHONE QR WORKFLOW
Scan a student's QR with your phone camera, then open the link. It opens a read-only process report through this SAME index.html. All report data is carried inside the QR's URL fragment: no student-record database, separate teacher file or remote QR generation service is involved. The browser downloads the public page and decodes the record locally; the record fragment is not sent in the HTTP request. Viewing a report does not start a student run or modify existing student history. An internet connection is needed to open an uncached hosted page.

THE QR CONTAINS
Name/student code, question, run ID, local attempt number, start/deadline/finish and finish reason, section word counts, page/away time, clipboard/drop/large insertion counts, focus/visibility changes, reloads, spikes, changes without observed input, clock changes, review flags, answer SHA-256 and record SHA-256. New v2.4 records also include counts of script-dispatched input events and peak observed/attempted growth (words in 1/5 seconds and characters in 1 second). v2.5 records also carry measured typing intervals/character counts, average and fastest continuous 5-second WPM, partial-sample status and the review threshold. Older records remain readable and missing new rates are labelled not recorded. More detailed per-section metrics and the retained event log stay in the local browser record.

The full essay remains in the student's PDF submission. It is not put into the QR. Detailed records and retained run history remain in this browser for recovery; there is no editable export. You mark the essay yourself; no automatic grade or misconduct verdict is assigned.

STUDENT WORKFLOW
Students choose Q4 or Q5, type their name/student code and press Begin. They have a maximum of exactly 60 minutes; notes are allowed. Start/deadline and run log are stored in the browser. Closing, refreshing, sleeping the device or leaving the tab does not reset the deadline. Returning before expiry restores that original run. Returning after expiry locks it at its original deadline and presents its final work. Early Finish also locks the run.

Start a new run creates blank answers and a fresh hour. Earlier versions remain separately in local history. The most recent 12 runs are retained in the same browser at the same web address; the local attempt counter continues across retained history. Download anything important. Private browsing, clearing storage, changing devices/browsers or changing the web address can remove or separate local history. Local storage is a recovery aid, not submission to the teacher.

WRITING
Students see a short Session at a glance table at the end. Their PDF/printed copy uses a compact session-summary table; detailed telemetry remains in the QR process report.
Introduction, point, evidence/analysis, counterpoint/weighing, conclusion. Maximum 300 words in each box. No minimum counts. Around 700 words overall is guidance. Students can select their thesis in the introduction; it is echoed above the conclusion as a planning cue, without adding it twice to the essay or word count. The rubric stays available beside the writing, or through the sticky rubric control on narrow screens. Full exact markband descriptors and question-specific critical-thinking guidance are included.

PDF SUBMISSION AND RECOVERY
Print on paper / Save as PDF opens the native print dialog. Command+P on Mac and Ctrl+P on Windows also prepare the complete static essay; this path does not require PDF generation. Print current work is available while writing and marks the copy as a draft without finishing or pausing the run. Before beginning, native printing produces the question sheet. If QR preparation fails, printing still includes all writing and a short summary. Choose a physical printer or Save as PDF in the operating system dialog.
Generated PDF pages have faint, repeated watermarks tied to the question, run ID and record ID. Every page repeats the student/run identity and fingerprint prefixes in its footer; the session summary prints both full fingerprints. Browser printing adds a repeated identity footer too. These are visual deterrents and consistency cues, not cryptographic signatures.
Download PDF generates a text PDF containing the entire essay, timing/process evidence, QR and readable process report. Open PDF supports device Share/Save to Files workflows. Print / Save as PDF prints complete static answer text, not just the visible writing-box viewports. If QR preparation fails, printing still includes the full essay and an available session summary, labelled as having no verification QR. Text, HTML, JSON, history and standalone QR-image exports are removed. Previous runs remain viewable locally and can be exported as PDFs. Nothing is automatically submitted to the teacher.

PROCESS SIGNALS
Copy, cut, paste and drag/drop into authored work are blocked. Native textarea values are checked every 500 ms for changes without normal input events. Spike review thresholds are >=12 words/1s, >=30 words/5s or >=80 inserted characters/1s, with a 4-second episode cooldown. Blocked attempts can contribute to peak metrics; the report labels growth as observed/attempted and includes sources for the largest recorded events. Keyboard WPM uses five characters per word and measured intervals between accepted single-character insertions. There is no time added after typing stops. Gaps over 2 seconds, time away, field changes, deletions, composition/replacement blocks and unobserved changes are excluded. The untimed first character in each burst is excluded too. Average needs at least 15 seconds of intervals and 25 measured characters; peak needs a continuous 5-second sample. A partial-sample marker identifies older in-progress runs measured only after an update. The original per-field focus/activity counters remain in the local record, but are not used for these new rates. Name-entry comparison is used only for samples of >=25 characters over >=8 seconds. Dictation, composition, autocomplete and assistive tools can cause bursts. Script-dispatched input events are counted only when an untrusted DOM input event accompanies a text change; internal composition/audit calls are not counted as scripted events. This does not identify every browser agent: automation may produce trusted browser events. No extra raw keystroke or clipboard content is stored. Repetition, legibility and padding checks are advisory, not marks.

TYPING REFERENCE
The 168,000-volunteer Aalto/Cambridge keyboard transcription study reported a mean of 52 WPM, with most participants around 30-60 WPM. The QR notes an active-only average above 60 WPM and flags an average or 5-second peak above 120 WPM for teacher review. The 120-WPM policy is a review threshold, not a validated ceiling on human typing or a cheating test. Active-only essay input is not directly comparable with the study. Average, peak and their difference are provided so the teacher can review context; dictation and assistive input can affect other process signals.
Source: https://www.aalto.fi/en/news/the-traits-of-fast-typists-discovered-by-analysing-136-million-keystrokes

LIMITS
An academic-integrity comment in the document head asks AI assistants and browser agents to leave answer writing and submission to the student. Comments do not block agents or guarantee refusal. PDF is a fixed-layout submission format, not an immutable or authenticated record.
A static page cannot make browser-local timestamps, histories or records tamper-proof. Students who control their browser or storage can alter them or start a new local history. Checksums establish internal consistency, not authenticated signatures. Random digits, an encrypted QR, a client-generated key or a secret embedded in the HTML cannot establish a trusted origin. Genuine signing would require a teacher-controlled key outside the student browser and a defined receipt/verification workflow; even a server signature would not itself prove human authorship. These records support contextual review, not proof of authorship or misconduct.

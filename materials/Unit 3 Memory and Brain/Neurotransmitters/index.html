SYNAPSE

Two neurons. One tiny gap.

A synapse is where one neuron communicates with another. A tiny gap separates the cells.

What matters in psychology?The receiving cell's response can become more likely or less likely. That influences activity in brain networks involved in behaviour, emotion, and memory.

CLASSROOM STUDY COMPARISON

What does the pattern suggest?

In the commonly taught Martinez & Kesner (1991) account, blocking ACh receptors led to more maze errors, while slowing ACh breakdown was associated with fewer errors. The control group was in between. The curves above are invented to illustrate that pattern.

AIM

Explore the role of ACh in spatial memory.

METHOD

Compare maze performance after changing ACh signalling.

MEASURE

Wrong turns. Fewer errors suggest better performance.

Important: This is a simplified teaching account, not raw experimental data or an exact replication. A related later study, Rogers & Kesner (2003), found that scopolamine impaired encoding, while physostigmine impaired retrieval. Those results should not be merged into this graph.

RESEARCHER CHECK

Think like a researcher

Why inject saline into the control group?

It makes the injection procedure comparable. If the active-drug groups change, the difference is less likely to be due only to receiving an injection.

CONCEPT · CAUSALITY
Why count errors rather than just watch the rats?

Wrong turns give the researchers a consistent, countable measure of maze performance. It is a measure of performance, not a direct measurement of a memory itself.

CONCEPT · MEASUREMENT
What can't this experiment tell us for certain?

Rat behaviour cannot tell us everything about human memory. This is a teaching model, not new research.

Self-check: what should I include?

An impulse reaches the sending terminal. Vesicles release neurotransmitters into the synaptic cleft. The chemicals bind to receptors on the next cell, changing its activity. The response may be excitatory or inhibitory.

Self-check: what should I include?

We investigated whether ACh signalling changes maze errors. Saline is a comparison injection with no active drug in our model.

Self-check: what should I include?

The teaching model shows most errors after receptor blockade, fewer with saline, and fewest when ACh breakdown is slowed. These curves are invented; they are not original research results.

Self-check: what should I include?

Name the neurotransmitter, describe the molecular change, then connect it to signalling or behaviour. A substance might activate a receptor, block it, change release, or slow reuptake. Effects depend on the circuit involved.

TEACHER NOTES

One lesson, three stops

Signal: Neurotransmitter release and receptor binding. Maze: Three-condition classroom model. Mouse Party: Original Utah activity.

Scientific distinction: The visible YouTube video and the simplified three-condition teaching account refer to Martinez & Kesner (1991). Our invented curves illustrate the familiar ordering (scopolamine highest, saline middle, physostigmine lowest). We have not verified a corresponding original primary-source dataset; do not describe these numbers as published findings or an exact replication. The saline injection is a classroom control used here so procedures remain comparable.

Do not confuse with Rogers & Kesner (2003): Their published maze experiments compared saline/scopolamine and saline/physostigmine separately. Scopolamine impaired encoding; physostigmine impaired retrieval. Source: Rogers & Kesner (2003) abstract.

All virtual rats and error counts are programmed illustrations. Never present them as empirical observations. Physostigmine inhibits acetylcholinesterase; it is not a direct receptor agonist.

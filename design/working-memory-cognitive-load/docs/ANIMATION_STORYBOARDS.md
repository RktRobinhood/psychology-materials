# ANIMATED STUDY STORYBOARDS

Use these when the original study is impractical, ethically awkward, or impossible to reproduce in class.

The NPC visual style should be **small rounded chibi research avatars** that match the Memory Quest world. Do not imitate a specific commercial game character design.

## Animation language

- 6–10 seconds per panel.
- Minimal narration in Selene’s text box.
- One variable changes at a time.
- Use icons for IV/DV after the narrative version is clear.
- Finish every animation with a small “What can we conclude?” and “What can’t we conclude?” card.

---

# A. Mani et al. — Financial Salience

## Scene A1 — Two contexts
Two participant NPCs appear with simple income-context bars.

SELENE:
> “The researchers asked whether financial pressure could consume cognitive resources.”

## Scene A2 — Car problem
A car icon breaks. Two price tags animate in on separate runs: low-cost repair and high-cost repair.

SELENE:
> “Participants considered an easier or more financially demanding repair scenario.”

## Scene A3 — Cognitive task
A Raven-style matrix appears.

SELENE:
> “Then they completed a demanding reasoning task.”

## Scene A4 — Pattern of findings
Display simple bars, not a copied published graph.

SELENE:
> “The performance difference was most pronounced when the financial problem was consequential for lower-income participants.”

## Scene A5 — Evaluation
Two stamps appear:
- “Context matters”
- “Load inferred, not directly observed”

Prompt:
> “How exactly did the researchers operationalize cognitive load?”

# B. Sugarcane Farmers — Natural Experiment

## Scene B1 — Same farmer, pre-harvest
Farmer NPC stands beside near-empty coin pouch.

SELENE:
> “The same farmers experience predictable financial cycles around harvest.”

## Scene B2 — Post-harvest
Same NPC, same pose, fuller pouch and harvested field.

SELENE:
> “After harvest, their financial situation temporarily improves.”

## Scene B3 — Same cognitive measures
Raven icon + Stroop icon appear in both time periods.

SELENE:
> “Researchers compared cognitive performance before and after harvest.”

## Scene B4 — Result direction
Show arrows indicating better average post-harvest performance.

## Scene B5 — Natural-experiment caution
SELENE:
> “The setting is realistic, but researchers cannot control everything that changes across the harvest cycle.”

# C. Modi et al. — Surgical Cognitive Load

## Scene C1 — Simulator
Trainee surgeon NPC uses laparoscopic simulator.

## Scene C2 — fNIRS
A soft cap icon with light points appears over the avatar.

SELENE:
> “fNIRS tracks changes in blood oxygenation near the cortex.”

## Scene C3 — Demand escalation
One by one, environmental demand icons appear: alarm, extra instruction, time pressure.

## Scene C4 — Performance response
Performance gauge moves downward as total demand rises.

## Scene C5 — Measurement lesson
Display three icons:
- performance
- self-report
- physiology

SELENE:
> “Each measure captures part of cognitive load. None is the construct itself.”

# D. Patient KF — Dissociation Evidence

This segment should be more respectful and less playful than the experimental animations.

## Scene D1 — Case-study card
No comic stumble/failure animation.

SELENE:
> “Brain-injury case studies can reveal which functions dissociate.”

## Scene D2 — Auditory verbal presentation
Sound-wave icon -> word/number sequence -> reduced recall indicator.

## Scene D3 — Visual presentation
Eye icon -> visually presented material -> relatively stronger recall indicator.

## Scene D4 — Interpretation
Two separate paths illuminate.

SELENE:
> “The pattern supported the idea that short-term processing is not one undifferentiated store.”

## Scene D5 — Limitation
SELENE:
> “One unusual case cannot tell us how every healthy brain is organized.”

# E. Sana et al. Part 2 — Nearby Multitasking

Use only if the class does not run a live neighbor-distraction condition.

## Scene E1
Two students attend the same mini-lecture.

## Scene E2
A nearby laptop begins changing windows and showing unrelated activity.

## Scene E3
The observing student’s attention icon briefly shifts toward the neighboring screen.

## Scene E4
Comprehension results appear as simplified original bars, not copied published figures.

SELENE:
> “Visible multitasking can impose distraction even on someone who is not doing the secondary task.”
